Every drawing on this website is made of simple lines: circles, straight segments and arcs, placed one by one by short formulas. Most of them are the formulas published by the mathematical artist Hamid Naderi Yeganeh in “Making Mathematical Art” (Scientific American); the opening figure follows his animation 15,000 Moving Circles. The drawings are generated live in your browser. They keep moving slowly, lean a little toward the mouse pointer, and change when clicked.

In every formula k runs from 1 to N and s = k / N. On the website a slowly increasing time t is added inside some terms, so the figures flow; set t = 0 to get the still image.

15,000 moving circles — the opening

A four-fold curve wound 21 times, with a fast 13-fold loop riding on it that curls each chain of circles into small spirals. The colours pass through six sets in turn.

The small drawing beside “Read more” under each post: every point on a circle joined to the point at three times its angle. Together the lines trace a cardioid.

a = 2πk / 72, k = 1 … 72
line k: from (cos a, sin a) to (cos 3a, sin 3a)

The line portrait — About

104 horizontal lines drawn over a 68 × 110 grid of darkness values taken from a photograph (only the grid is stored, not the photo). Each line waves harder where the picture is darker, and a band of colour sweeps across all the lines at once. Click it for the second version: a halftone of circles whose size follows the same darkness.

d = darkness(x, y)^1.25, between 0 and 1
phase grows along the line by (0.1 + 1.1 d) per step
y_line(x) = y₀ + sin(phase + 3t) · d · 0.95 · gap
halftone: r = darkness^1.2 · 0.62 · cell
